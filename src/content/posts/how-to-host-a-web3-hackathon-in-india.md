Since 2024 we've hosted or co-hosted university workshop series, city meetups, hacker houses and hackathons across India. These include the [Polkadot India Workshops Series](/education/workshops/) (IIT Roorkee, IIT Bombay, MIT Manipal, NITK Surathkal), the Polkadot track at [ETHIndia 2024](/education/events/ethindia-2024-polkadot/), and the [AssetHub Hackathon](/education/events/assethub-hackathon-2025/) with its finale in Goa. Here is what we learned, taken from our own public post-event reports.

## 1. Pick the format for the goal
| Goal | Format that worked |
|---|---|
| First exposure for students | Two-day university workshop series (six sessions) |
| Converting learners into builders | Online eliminator round followed by an in-person finale |
| Shipping real projects | A 2.5–4 day hacker house with accommodation and mentors |
| Ecosystem awareness at scale | A booth, a workshop and judging at a large hackathon (ETHIndia) |

## 2. Make workshops two days, not one
At IIT Roorkee every session ran over time. Students asked more questions than we planned for. Since then, our workshops run over **two days**, with built-in buffer time.

## 3. Start promotion four weeks out
More students turned up than registered, through word of mouth. With a longer runway we could have booked a larger hall. Now we promote for **four weeks or more**, and we partner with the university's own tech club (WnCC at IIT Bombay, IECSE at Manipal, WebClub and COSH at NITK).

## 4. Activities beat lectures
Our two best-rated activities were *Rob the Bank* (students create forks by spending from shared accounts, and the longest chain wins) and a *Decryption Challenge*. A competitive game makes Merkle trees and consensus stick.

## 5. Give hackers one page that explains everything
At ETHIndia 2024 we put a **Hackathon Bible** on the booth. It covered the five ways to deploy on Polkadot: ink!, Frontier EVMs, pallet-revive, pallets and runtimes. **22 projects** entered the Polkadot track, and 40.9% chose non-EVM routes.

## 6. Use POAPs for engagement
At IIT Roorkee all 200 POAPs were claimed. We placed the POAP kiosk between workshops to keep people in the room.

## 7. Budget for merch shortfalls and late deliveries
At ETHIndia our notebooks arrived late in batches, so we handed them out at the booth instead. At Roorkee we ran out of T-shirts. Carry a buffer.

## 8. Return what you don't spend, and say so publicly
Every report we file lists the unused funds we returned (for example, 1,723.992 DOT after ETHIndia and 291 DOT after IIT Bombay), with on-chain links. Doing this consistently is how sponsors learn to trust an organiser.

## 9. Keep mentoring after the closing ceremony
Our AssetHub Hackathon winner, the PolkaZK Verifier SDK, went on into post-hackathon [curation](/business/curations/zk-verifier-sdk-zkama/). Events should feed into longer-term support for the teams.
